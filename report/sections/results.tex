\section{Statistical Analysis Framework}
\label{sec:statistical-framework}

The statistical analysis framework draws upon several key measures to evaluate performance differences between model variants. Means are reported using the conventional notation M with standard deviations (STD), while 95\% confidence intervals capture the range of plausible population values using the standard error multiplied by 1.96.

ANOVA serves as the primary analytical approach for comparing multiple groups while maintaining appropriate control over Type I error rates. The F-statistic follows the notation F(a,b), where degrees of freedom reflect the between-group comparison (a = groups - 1) and within-group variability (b = total observations - groups). With three optimization variants and 750 total evaluations, this yields F(2,747). The analysis tests whether all group means are equivalent ($H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2 = \mu_3$) against the alternative of at least one differing mean.

Practical significance receives equal attention through effect size quantification. The $\eta^2$ statistic captures the proportion of total variance attributable to group differences, calculated as $\text{SS}_{\text{between}}/\text{SS}_{\text{total}}$. Cohen's (1988) guidelines establish $\eta^2$ values of 0.01, 0.06, and 0.14 as small, medium, and large effects respectively. Pairwise comparisons utilize Cohen's d, computed as $(M_1 - M_2)/s_{\text{pooled}}$, with thresholds of $|d| = 0.2$, 0.5, and 0.8 marking small, medium, and large standardized differences.

P-values indicate the probability of obtaining observed results (or more extreme outcomes) under the null hypothesis of no group differences. Statistical significance follows the conventional $\alpha = 0.05$ threshold, while non-significant results (p $\geq$ 0.05) suggest insufficient evidence for meaningful group differences.

\section{Empirical Overview}
\label{sec:empirical-overview}

Three model variants (Baseline, DPO-Synthetic, DPO-Hybrid) were examined using a balanced design with 250 email evaluations per condition. The omnibus ANOVA tested for significant differences among optimization approaches, yielding F(2,747) = 0.329, well below the critical threshold of F$_{crit}$ = 3.02 required for significance at $\alpha = 0.05$. With p = 0.720, there is a 72\% probability of observing such differences under the null hypothesis of equal means.

The corresponding effect size of $\eta^2 = 0.001$ shows that optimization approach explains only 0.1\% of total performance variance, falling far below Cohen's small effect threshold of 1\%. Combined with the non-significant F-test, this negligible effect size supports statistical equivalence rather than meaningful differences between optimization variants.

The balanced design ensured equal representation across all three variants ($N = 250$ each), with comprehensive evaluation coverage across all 50 validation topics. No missing observations were encountered, providing robust statistical analysis with enhanced power relative to incomplete designs. Score distributions spanned the full performance range with comparable variability patterns across variants, enhancing the generalizability of findings and eliminating potential biases from incomplete data collection.

\section{Effect Size Quantification}
\label{sec:effect-size-analysis}

All pairwise comparisons produced uniformly negligible effect sizes, with Cohen's d values falling well below the conventional small effect threshold of $|d| = 0.2$. The largest effect was d = -0.065 for Baseline vs DPO-Synthetic, representing only 6.5\% of one standard deviation difference and approximately 32 times smaller than Cohen's small effect criterion.

All effect size confidence intervals encompass zero, indicating no reliable directional preferences between optimization approaches. The Baseline vs DPO-Synthetic comparison produced d = -0.065 (95\% CI [-0.240, 0.111]), spanning a range that includes both small negative and positive effects. Baseline vs DPO-Hybrid yielded d = -0.056 (95\% CI [-0.231, 0.119]), while DPO-Synthetic vs DPO-Hybrid produced d = 0.011 (95\% CI [-0.164, 0.187]).

These effect sizes correspond to distributions with approximately 97\%, 98\%, and 99\% shared area under the curve, respectively. This minimal separation indicates that optimization variants produce nearly identical performance distributions, with differences indistinguishable from measurement error in the evaluation framework (Figure~\ref{fig:effect-size-forest}).

Despite these domain-specific variations, the overall statistical equivalence indicates that category-level heterogeneity was insufficient to overcome the population-level null effect, with domain-specific improvements offset by corresponding decreases across the complete evaluation framework.
