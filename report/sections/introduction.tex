\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Background}

The evolution of artificial intelligence has increasingly shifted toward collaborative architectures where specialized agents work together to accomplish complex tasks \cite{guo2024llm_multiagent}. Multi-agent systems offer unique advantages in modularity, specialization, and robustness, enabling AI frameworks that adapt to complex, domain-specific requirements while maintaining interpretability \cite{ma2024agentboard}. Natural language generation has emerged as a particularly promising application for multi-agent architectures, especially in domains requiring sophisticated communication and contextual understanding.

Email generation represents a challenging domain within natural language processing due to requirements for personalized tone, appropriate formality, contextual relevance, and persuasive effectiveness \cite{zhang2019email_subject}. Traditional email automation systems have focused primarily on efficiency rather than sophisticated content quality assessment. However, as organizations increasingly rely on automated communication, demand has grown for more effective email generation systems with comprehensive evaluation methodologies \cite{li2023generative_judge}.

Preference optimization techniques have emerged as promising approaches for aligning AI systems with human preferences in communication tasks \cite{rafailov2023dpo}. However, the effectiveness of these optimization strategies within multi-agent frameworks remains largely unexplored, particularly when constrained by limited training data typical of specialized domains.

Evaluation of multi-agent systems presents unique methodological challenges that differ significantly from single-model assessment approaches \cite{yehudai2025survey_llm_agents}. Traditional metrics such as BLEU and ROUGE fail to capture collaborative dynamics, inter-agent communication effectiveness, and emergent behaviors characterizing multi-agent performance \cite{liu2023geval}. Furthermore, email generation evaluation requires domain-specific metrics assessing pragmatic effectiveness beyond linguistic quality \cite{li2023generative_judge}. Current evaluation frameworks exhibit significant biases and inconsistencies that undermine objective assessment, particularly problematic for real-world deployment scenarios \cite{wang2025judging_bias_lrms}.

\section{Aims and Objectives}

This research addresses critical gaps at the intersection of multi-agent systems, preference optimization, and email generation evaluation. The primary aim is to develop and evaluate a comprehensive multi-agent framework for email generation that integrates preference optimization techniques with novel evaluation methodologies.

The specific research objectives are: First, to investigate how different preference optimization strategies perform within multi-agent email generation frameworks, particularly when constrained by limited training data typical of specialized domains \cite{feng2024dpo_limitations}. Second, to develop a standardized evaluation framework for multi-agent systems that addresses the significant methodological gaps hindering systematic comparison of different optimization approaches \cite{li2024generation_to_judgment}. Third, to establish empirical foundations for understanding multi-agent system behavior under different optimization conditions and provide practical guidance for practitioners implementing similar systems in resource-constrained environments.

The research specifically examines three preference optimization variants—Baseline, DPO-Synthetic, and DPO-Hybrid—within a structured three-agent architecture comprising Email Generator, Checklist Creator, and Judge Agent components. Through systematic evaluation using both traditional metrics and novel reasoning-enhanced approaches, this work establishes the comparative effectiveness of different optimization strategies while developing bias-resistant evaluation protocols transferable across diverse collaborative AI applications.

Chapter 5 discusses implications for multi-agent system design, preference optimization strategies, and evaluation methodologies, integrating empirical results with broader theoretical considerations and practical applications. The discussion addresses research limitations and identifies future investigation directions.

The concluding chapter synthesizes key contributions and establishes significance within the evolving landscape of multi-agent artificial intelligence systems, emphasizing transferability of methodological innovations and practical implications for practitioners working with constrained training data.
