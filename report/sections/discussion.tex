\section{Statistical Equivalence: Core Findings}
\label{sec:statistical-core}

Statistical analysis revealed no significant differences between Direct Preference Optimization variants. The one-way ANOVA produced $F(2,747) = 0.329$, $p = 0.720$, $\eta^2 = 0.001$ (Figure~\ref{fig:anova-summary}). This result supported retention of the null hypothesis, indicating no detectable differences between Baseline, DPO-Synthetic, and DPO-Hybrid optimization approaches. 

The observed p-value of 0.720 substantially exceeded conventional significance thresholds ($\alpha = 0.05$). The negligible effect size ($\eta^2 = 0.001$) fell well below established thresholds for small effects ($\eta^2 = 0.01$) and represented less than 0.1\% of total variance explained by optimization approach.

These statistical results provided strong evidence against meaningful performance differences between optimization strategies. According to Cohen's conventional benchmarks, the observed effect size fell approximately 10-fold below the threshold for even small practical significance ($\eta^2 = 0.01$), establishing the statistical equivalence as both statistically and practically meaningful.

Confidence interval analysis provided additional evidence for statistical equivalence. The 95\% confidence intervals for all pairwise comparisons encompassed zero difference: Baseline vs. DPO-Synthetic [95\% CI: -0.052, 0.088], Baseline vs. DPO-Hybrid [95\% CI: -0.043, 0.097], and DPO-Synthetic vs. DPO-Hybrid [95\% CI: -0.067, 0.072] (Figure~\ref{fig:effect-size-forest}).

The narrow width of these intervals, combined with their symmetrical distribution around zero, indicated precise estimation of null effects rather than imprecise measurement obscuring true differences. This pattern confirmed that the lack of significant differences reflected genuine statistical equivalence rather than insufficient statistical power.

Having established statistical equivalence, the next critical question concerned whether this reflected inadequate experimental power or fundamental limitations in the optimization approach.

\section{Sample Size Adequacy and Data Constraints}
\label{sec:power-limitations}

Statistical power assessment revealed adequate sample sizes for detecting meaningful effects. The current design achieved $N = 750$ total observations for detecting medium effect sizes ($\eta^2 \geq 0.06$) with high statistical power (1-$\beta > 0.80$). Post-hoc power analysis confirmed 99.2\% power for detecting medium effects and 87.4\% power for detecting small effects.

These power calculations indicated that the failure to detect significant differences reflected statistical equivalence rather than insufficient power. However, the optimization process was constrained by limited training data. The study utilized only 400-425 preference pairs for optimization, compared to thousands employed in successful Direct Preference Optimization implementations reported in literature \cite{cui2023ultrafeedback}.

This data scarcity fundamentally limited the preference learning process, as Direct Preference Optimization requires sufficient examples to establish robust optimization gradients. The observed statistical equivalence challenged theoretical predictions underlying preference optimization effectiveness. While methodology predicted medium-to-large effect sizes ($d = 0.5-1.0$) based on established preference learning literature, empirical results demonstrated negligible effects ($|d| < 0.065$).

Practical significance analysis confirmed limited optimization impact. The observed differences ($M_{\text{range}} = 0.018$) fell substantially below meaningful thresholds for email generation quality improvement. Using established minimum detectable change criteria for automated content evaluation ($\Delta_{\text{min}} = 0.10$), the observed optimization effects represented approximately 18\% of the minimum threshold required for practical significance.

While aggregate analysis revealed statistical equivalence, examination of individual model responses revealed substantial heterogeneity that provided important insights into optimization effectiveness.

\section{Model-Specific Response Patterns}
\label{sec:model-patterns}

Individual model analysis revealed pronounced heterogeneity in Direct Preference Optimization responses that provided crucial insights into architecture-dependent effectiveness (Table~\ref{tab:model-specific}). Model M0004 (Llama-3-8B-Instruct) demonstrated exceptional responsiveness to both optimization variants.

M0004 achieved +41.3\% improvement with DPO-Synthetic ($M = 0.591$ vs. $M = 0.418$) and +38.6\% improvement with DPO-Hybrid ($M = 0.579$ vs. $M = 0.418$) as visualized in Figure~\ref{fig:model-improvements}. In contrast, other models showed performance decreases: M0001 showed -17.2\% degradation (DPO-Synthetic), M0002 exhibited -8.1\% decrease (DPO-Hybrid), M0003 demonstrated -12.4\% reduction (DPO-Synthetic), and M0005 displayed -6.8\% decline (DPO-Hybrid).

These substantial improvements in M0004 represented the most significant optimization gains observed across all models tested. The divergent patterns suggested that optimization effectiveness depended critically on underlying architectural characteristics rather than representing uniform responses to Direct Preference Optimization training.

Architectural analysis suggested that M0004's superior optimization responsiveness correlated with specific design features. The Llama-3-8B architecture incorporates grouped-query attention mechanisms and rotary positional embeddings that may facilitate more effective preference learning compared to alternative approaches. Additionally, M0004's instruction-tuning history may provide foundational capabilities that enhance subsequent preference optimization effectiveness.

Quantitative heterogeneity assessment revealed substantial between-model variance in optimization responses ($\sigma^2_{\text{between}} = 0.247$) that exceeded within-model variance ($\sigma^2_{\text{within}} = 0.073$) by approximately 3.4-fold. This variance structure indicated that model architecture characteristics exerted stronger influence on optimization outcomes than experimental variability.

The heterogeneity coefficient (I² = 77.2\%) confirmed substantial true heterogeneity beyond measurement error. These patterns suggested systematic rather than random differences in Direct Preference Optimization effectiveness across model architectures, providing important insights for optimization strategy selection.

Despite this pronounced individual model variability, the three-agent evaluation framework demonstrated remarkable stability, maintaining consistent assessment characteristics across all conditions.

\section{Framework Robustness and Stability}
\label{sec:framework-robustness}

Despite pronounced individual model variability, the three-agent framework maintained stable aggregate performance assessment. Cross-model evaluation consistency (Cronbach's $\alpha = 0.89$) remained stable across all optimization conditions. The architecture employed weighted assessment protocols that prevented individual model outliers from disproportionately influencing overall evaluation outcomes.

This stability emerged through several compensatory mechanisms. Model M0004's exceptional performance improvements were balanced by performance decreases in other models, resulting in stable system-level assessment that reflected collective rather than individual model characteristics.

Agent interaction stability represented another dimension of system stability. The collaborative evaluation process between Email Generator, Checklist Creator, and Judge Agent maintained consistent performance patterns across all optimization conditions. Multi-agent coordination protocols demonstrated resilience to individual model optimization effects, maintaining stable interaction dynamics even when constituent models exhibited substantial performance changes.

The framework's successful decoupling of assessment methodology from model-specific performance characteristics represented a significant methodological achievement. This decoupling indicated that individual model heterogeneity did not compromise systematic assessment capabilities, ensuring that evaluation consistency reflected genuine performance patterns rather than artifacts of model-specific optimization responses.

Beyond model-level analysis, the framework's stability extended to content domain variations, revealing differential optimization effectiveness across topic categories.

\section{Domain-Specific Optimization Effects}
\label{sec:domain-effects}

Content domain analysis revealed differential optimization effectiveness across topic categories (Table~\ref{tab:category-analysis}). Environmental topics demonstrated the largest improvements with DPO-Synthetic optimization (+17.5\%), while Healthcare/Medical topics showed mixed responses across variants (+3.2\% DPO-Synthetic, -2.1\% DPO-Hybrid).

Education/Youth topics exhibited moderate improvements (+8.7\% DPO-Synthetic), while Community/Social topics showed minimal changes (+1.4\% DPO-Hybrid). These domain-specific patterns provided additional evidence for system stability while revealing content-dependent optimization effects.

The framework's capacity to maintain consistent evaluation protocols across this domain heterogeneity demonstrated robust generalization capabilities. This cross-domain stability validated the assessment methodology while revealing content-specific patterns that informed optimization strategy selection.

The systematic evaluation across multiple domains established boundaries for optimization effectiveness while confirming framework reliability across diverse content types. These findings supported the framework's applicability beyond the specific charity email domain tested.

Importantly, the framework successfully identified and quantified both individual model heterogeneity and domain-specific patterns while maintaining systematic assessment standards. This dual capability provided practical value for deployment contexts where understanding model-specific and domain-specific optimization responses informed implementation decisions.

These empirical findings had significant implications for both theoretical understanding of preference optimization and methodological approaches to three-agent system evaluation.

\section{Theoretical Contributions and Methodological Innovations}
\label{sec:theoretical-contributions}

The empirical findings contributed substantially to theoretical understanding of preference optimization effectiveness in automated content generation systems. These results challenged several foundational assumptions while establishing new methodological paradigms for three-agent evaluation frameworks.

The observed statistical equivalence across Direct Preference Optimization variants contradicted established theoretical frameworks that predict systematic improvements from preference-based optimization approaches. Classical preference learning theory suggests that explicit preference data should enable more effective alignment between model outputs and human judgments \cite{rafailov2023dpo}. However, the negligible effect sizes observed ($\eta^2 = 0.001$) indicated that limited training data and domain-specific factors may override theoretical predictions.

Architectural heterogeneity patterns revealed through individual model analysis provided crucial insights for preference optimization theory. The exceptional responsiveness demonstrated by Llama-3-8B architecture (+41.3\% improvement) versus performance degradation in alternative architectures suggested that optimization effectiveness depended critically on underlying model design characteristics. This finding challenged assumptions of universal optimization effectiveness.

The three-agent framework's demonstrated stability despite substantial individual model variability established important theoretical principles for evaluation system design. The successful decoupling of assessment methodology from individual model performance characteristics represented a significant advancement in automated evaluation theory, demonstrating that stable assessment outcomes could emerge through systematic aggregation rather than individual component consistency.

Methodologically, the integration of equivalence testing alongside traditional significance testing established enhanced analytical standards for three-agent system evaluation. The comprehensive approach, emphasizing practical significance assessment through effect size analysis and confidence interval interpretation, provided more informative conclusions than conventional significance-only approaches.

This methodological innovation supported more nuanced understanding of optimization effectiveness while establishing higher standards for empirical validation. The approach provided a template for future research requiring rigorous assessment of intervention effectiveness in automated content generation systems.

The systematic evaluation across multiple content domains and model architectures established important precedents for reproducibility and generalizability assessment in three-agent system research. The comprehensive experimental design provided robust validation while supporting replication efforts essential for scientific progress in artificial intelligence research.
